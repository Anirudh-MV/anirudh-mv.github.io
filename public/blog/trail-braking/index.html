Why Trail Brake the Bike Here:

Keeping 54% front brake compresses the forks, shortening wheelbase and steepening rake angle so the bike points into the turn effortlessly. If you released the brake abruptly at turn-in, the forks would rebound ("pogo"), widening your radius and shrinking the front tire contact patch right when you need grip most!

Why Trail Brake the Car Here:

Trailing 68% brake pins heavy vertical load onto the front axle (killing entry understeer) while unweighting the rear axle so the tail pivots around the nose toward the apex. Unlike a bike, if the front tire slightly exceeds 100% grip, it merely scrubs a few degrees of slip angle rather than crashing.

Backing Research & Citations

A textbook “friction circle” assumes a vehicle trades 100% longitudinal braking for 100% lateral cornering along a clean circular boundary. Empirical MotoGP/WorldSBK and GT telemetry show adherence envelopes that are anything but circular — and radically different between two and four wheels. Every trace and readout in this dashboard is mapped to the primary vehicle-dynamics literature and empirical datasets below.

Bike G-G Envelope

Cossalter, Lot & Massaro (2011/2014) — An interpretative model of g-g diagrams of racing motorcycles, Proc. IMechE Part D.

Heart/teardrop boundary: upright braking is pitch-limited by rear-wheel lift (stoppie threshold ≈ −1.25 G street/track, −1.55 G with MotoGP aero), and roll coupling forces braking capacity to decay quasi-linearly toward 55° of lean.

Car G-G Envelope

Milliken & Milliken (1995) — Race Car Vehicle Dynamics (SAE) & Romanowski (2018) — TrailBrake GT3/Formula telemetry.

Mushroom boundary: low hcg/L ratio prevents pitch-over, while aerodynamic downforce (F ∝ v²) expands the high-speed braking top of the envelope (−1.6 to −2.0 G).

Brake Rise Rate (10–22%)

Code (2010) & Nick Ienatsch / ChampSchool (2019) — Brake-pressure transducer telemetry studies.

A bike needs a 0.25–0.40 s progressive ramp so the forks (75–95 mm travel), rake (∼2.2°) and trail (∼18 mm) can settle before peak pressure. Cars spike under 0.1 s to maximize aero braking.

Combined-Slip Taper (22–65%)

Pacejka (2012) — Tire and Vehicle Dynamics, 3rd Ed., Ch. 11 (MF-MCTyre vs MF-Tyre).

Camber thrust (γ ≤ 55°) with tiny slip angles (α < 3.5°) on bikes vs. slip-angle (4–8°) cars. Combined-slip weighting drops steeply on the tire shoulder: bikes bleed brake at ∼1.5–1.8% per degree of lean added.

Minimum-Lap-Time Trajectory (35–70%)

Massaro & Limebeer (2021) — Minimum-Lap-Time Control of Motorcycles / Vehicles, Vehicle System Dynamics.

Free-trajectory optimal control: bikes shed 80%+ of peak brake before 45° lean to avoid steering-torque tuck; cars hold 25–40% deep to the apex to pin the front axle and rotate.

Additional insight from Cossalter’s follow-up work: peak acceleration on high-power bikes occurs at moderate lean (∼15–25°), not upright — leaning lowers the effective center of mass and delays the wheelie limit. Contact-tracing of tire-shoulder combined slip is modeled on Pacejka’s MF-MCTyre high-camber formulation.

Core Mechanical & Physics Differences: Motorcycle vs. Car Trail Braking

Why you cannot brake a motorcycle like a car at corner entry
1. Initial Brake Application

Car: Violent initial stomp (0→100% in 0.1s) to utilize aerodynamic downforce at high speed.
Bike: Progressive squeeze (first 5% loads forks, then ramp to 100%). Grabbing 100% with uncompressed forks locks the lightly-loaded front tire immediately.

2. Steering Geometry Change

Car: Wheelbase is essentially fixed; trail braking shifts vertical weight onto front tires.
Bike: Compressing telescopic forks physically reduces rake & trail and shortens wheelbase—turning a stable straight-line missile into a sharp-turning chassis.

3. Tire Profile & Camber Thrust

Car: Wide flat tires turn via slip angle across 4 contact patches.
Bike: Parabolic crown tires turn via camber thrust at up to 60° lean. At max lean, the bike is on the very edge of the tire where longitudinal braking slows rotation and folds the front.

4. Penalty of Over-Braking

Car: Exceeding 100% traction while trail braking causes mild front understeer scrub or controllable oversteer rotation.
Bike: Exceeding 100% front traction at 45°+ lean tucked under brake load results in an instantaneous lowside crash.
